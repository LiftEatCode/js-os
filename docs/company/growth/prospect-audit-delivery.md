# Prospect Growth Audit Delivery v1

Status: **Testable sales deliverable v1**

## Purpose

Define the prospect-facing version of the JS Solutions Growth Audit used after a qualified prospect asks to see the review offered in outreach.

This is not a generic SEO report and not a comprehensive technical audit. Its job is to help a business owner understand:

> **What appears to be preventing this business from generating more of the right customers online, what evidence supports that diagnosis, and what should be prioritized next?**

The audit should create value even if the prospect never buys.

## Delivery principles

1. Lead with the business problem, not marketing terminology.
2. Use public, verifiable evidence.
3. Separate observed facts from reasonable inference.
4. Prioritize 3–5 opportunities rather than listing everything that could be changed.
5. Explain why an issue may matter before recommending work.
6. Preserve what the business already does well.
7. Never manufacture urgency, traffic estimates, rankings, lead counts, revenue impact, or competitor facts.
8. Do not assign a vanity overall grade unless the grade has a validated decision purpose.
9. Do not pitch a retainer inside every finding.
10. End with a business conversation, not a checkout button.

## Prospect-facing structure

### 1. Header

**JS Solutions — Local Growth Review**

Business: **[Business Name]**  
Market: **[Primary Market]**  
Prepared: **[Date]**

Public-information review. No private analytics, account access, or internal business data was used unless explicitly stated.

### 2. Executive diagnosis

Maximum roughly 100–150 words.

Answer:
- What is already strong?
- What is the central growth gap?
- Where would JS Solutions look first?

Example pattern:

> **[Business]** already has **[credible strength: reputation, owner story, experience, specialization, etc.]**. The main gap I found is that **[owned search/website/customer journey weakness]** does not communicate or capture that strength as effectively as it could. The highest-priority opportunities are **[A]**, **[B]**, and **[C]**. I would address those before expanding into lower-impact marketing activity.

Do not begin with a list of everything wrong with the company.

### 3. Top opportunities

Use 3–5 findings. Each finding follows the same decision structure.

#### Opportunity [N] — [Business-language title]

**Evidence**  
What was publicly observed. Include source/page/context where useful.

**Why it may matter**  
Explain the plausible customer-acquisition consequence. Use appropriately qualified language when causality is not directly measurable.

**Recommended action**  
What should be changed or investigated next.

**Priority**  
High / Medium / Low

**Confidence**  
High / Medium / Low

**Effort**  
Low / Medium / High

Optional when useful:
- Screenshot/reference
- competitor contrast
- validation needed after analytics/access

### 4. Competitive/customer decision snapshot

Keep this short and evidence-based.

Answer:
- What does a potential customer see when evaluating this company?
- What credible strengths are competitors communicating better?
- Where is this company already stronger?

Do not turn this into a generic competitor feature matrix.

### 5. Recommended first 30 days

Show sequencing, not a contract scope.

Example:

**First priority** — fix trust/conversion friction that can affect existing visitors.  
**Second priority** — strengthen the highest-value service/customer-intent paths.  
**Third priority** — improve local relevance/visibility architecture where evidence supports it.  
**Then measure** — establish analytics/conversion baselines and use actual demand data to determine the next backlog.

The plan should change based on the audit. Do not force every prospect into identical tasks.

### 6. What requires private data to validate

Explicitly state what cannot be concluded from public evidence.

Common examples:
- actual organic/local rankings across a service area;
- traffic volume and source mix;
- call/form conversion rate;
- lead quality;
- booked-job rate;
- revenue by service;
- Google Business Profile performance data;
- Search Console queries/impressions/clicks;
- analytics events;
- current agency/vendor work;
- operational capacity.

This section increases credibility by making the audit's limits visible.

### 7. Conversation CTA

Default close:

> If these priorities line up with what you're trying to improve, the next useful step would be a short conversation about which services/customers you actually want more of, what is already working, and whether the opportunity is large enough to justify doing anything further.

No artificial deadline. No guaranteed outcome.

## Finding quality test

A finding should normally satisfy:

**Evidence → Business consequence → Recommended action**

Weak:
> Your title tag is too long.

Better:
> The page targeting a high-value service uses a broad title that does not clearly communicate the service + market intent visible on the page. That can make the search result less aligned with what a customer is looking for. Rewrite the title/description around the actual service and location proposition, then validate impressions/click-through in Search Console once access exists.

Weak:
> You need more content.

Better:
> The site lists **[high-value service]** but gives customers little service-specific information before asking them to call. A dedicated service journey could answer common buying questions, establish relevant proof, and give search engines a clearer page for that intent. Build the page only if the service is economically important to the business.

## Audit scoring

Do not use a single 0–100 score as the primary prospect-facing conclusion.

If internal prioritization is useful, score individual opportunities on:
- impact;
- confidence;
- effort;
- business relevance;
- optional revenue potential when supported by client data.

Internal scoring exists to prioritize decisions, not manufacture precision for the prospect.

## Delivery format

During validation, the audit may be delivered as:
- concise email + structured document;
- PDF generated from the approved template;
- web-based report later when productized.

The content standard stays the same regardless of presentation layer.

Aim for a report an owner can understand in roughly 5–10 minutes.

## Audit-to-discovery transition

The audit earns the conversation. It does not replace discovery.

After delivery, ask:

> If these are worth improving, is generating more of the right local customer demand something you're actively working on right now?

If yes, discovery should determine:
- desired services/customer types;
- economics/value;
- capacity;
- existing acquisition sources;
- current marketing/vendors;
- measurement;
- urgency/timing;
- decision process;
- whether JS Solutions is actually the right fit.

Only then should an engagement be recommended.

## Future JS OS use

This structure is the human-validated contract for a future Growth Audit workflow. A future agent should be able to gather evidence and prepare findings, but unsupported claims, external delivery, and consequential recommendations remain subject to the JS OS evidence, tool, permission, policy, and approval model.
