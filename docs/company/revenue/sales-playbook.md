# Sales Playbook v1

**Status:** Testable operating system v1

## Philosophy

Diagnose before prescribing. Sales determines whether JS Solutions can create enough value for a mutually profitable engagement.

The first goal is not scale. It is to prove a repeatable path from qualified stranger to profitable retained client.

## Sales Flow

**Discover → Score → Research → Audit → Outreach → Follow-up → Conversation → Discovery → Proposal → Won/Lost/Nurture**

## Initial Hunting Grounds

Prioritize Tier A/B prospects in:

1. auto repair;
2. HVAC;
3. plumbing;
4. roofing;
5. electrical.

Do not permanently niche until outcome data supports it.

## Qualification

Use separate Fit, Opportunity, Economic Value, Capacity, Buying Signal, Contactability, and Proof Leverage dimensions. Do not waste deep research on hard-disqualified prospects.

## Research

Research enough to find a real, defensible observation. Tier A prospects justify a deeper mini-audit before contact. Generic personalization does not count.

## Outreach

Use:

**specific observation → potential business consequence → low-friction permission CTA**

The first touch should usually earn permission to send/discuss the audit rather than attempt to close a retainer.

## Audit Presentation

Lead with 3–5 meaningful opportunities, not dozens of technical defects.

For each:

**Evidence → Business consequence → Priority → Approach**

## Discovery

Understand:

- growth goals;
- priority/high-value services;
- service areas;
- current lead sources;
- capacity;
- prior/current marketing vendors;
- measurement maturity;
- decision process;
- urgency;
- customer economics.

## Offer

Default reference point for an appropriate single-location business:

**Local Growth System — $1,500/month**

Use a separate Growth Foundation project when initial corrective work materially exceeds recurring capacity. Use Growth Plus/custom scope for higher-complexity engagements.

## Proposal

Include:

- current situation;
- objectives;
- highest-value opportunities;
- recommended scope;
- first 30 days;
- measurement;
- responsibilities;
- exclusions;
- investment/terms;
- next step.

Price should not first appear as a surprise in the proposal.

## Objections

Record the actual category:

- price;
- timing;
- trust/proof;
- prior agency experience;
- guarantee request;
- internal resources;
- unclear value;
- no capacity;
- incumbent vendor;
- missing decision maker;
- scope mismatch.

Do not automatically discount. Prefer changing scope or timing when appropriate.

## Follow-Up

Add value or clarify a decision. Stop on opt-out, disqualification, inappropriate fit, or exhaustion of the defined sequence. Move long-horizon fits to nurture.

## First Validation Sprint

Build a prospect list large enough to produce approximately **100 genuinely qualified personalized outreach attempts**, rather than 100 scraped names.

Suggested initial vertical allocation:

- 30% auto repair;
- 25% HVAC;
- 20% plumbing;
- 15% roofing;
- 10% electrical.

Track every stage and time cost.

Do not treat the following as permanent benchmarks; they are diagnostic hypotheses:

- if ~100 genuinely qualified personalized outreaches produce <3% positive response, inspect targeting, evidence, message, channel, deliverability, and offer before increasing volume;
- if qualified conversations occur but proposals rarely follow, inspect qualification/discovery/value articulation;
- if ~20 genuinely qualified sales conversations produce <10% wins, inspect offer, trust/proof, pricing, sales execution, and market selection;
- if clients win but churn early or consume excessive hours, fix fulfillment/economics before scaling acquisition.

## Learning Loop

For every win/loss/nurture record:

- vertical;
- source;
- qualification dimensions;
- opportunity type;
- outreach angle;
- response;
- conversation result;
- objection;
- proposal amount;
- outcome;
- win/loss reason;
- sales-cycle duration;
- time spent.

For won clients, connect sales data to delivery hours, margin, retention, expansion, and measurable client results.

The eventual JS OS Sales Department should automate and optimize this validated process—not invent a different one.
