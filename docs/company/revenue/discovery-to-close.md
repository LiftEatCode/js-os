# Discovery to Close Operating System v1

**Status:** Testable operating system v1

## Purpose

Turn an engaged qualified prospect into a clear decision without pressure, premature proposals, or selling work that JS Solutions cannot economically deliver.

The operating flow is:

**Engaged → Discovery → Qualified Decision → Recommendation → Proposal → Decision → Won / Lost / Nurture**

A meeting, audit request, or positive reply is not a sale and does not automatically justify a proposal.

## 1. Pre-discovery preparation

Before the conversation, review:
- qualification score and hard-disqualifier status;
- original outreach and response;
- prospect research and audit;
- highest-confidence 3–5 findings;
- likely priority services/markets;
- verified decision maker(s);
- known incumbent providers;
- unresolved assumptions;
- likely implementation complexity.

Prepare hypotheses, not conclusions.

## 2. Discovery objective

By the end of discovery, JS Solutions should be able to answer:

1. What does the business want to grow?
2. Why does that matter economically?
3. How are customers currently acquired?
4. What appears to be limiting growth?
5. Can the business handle more demand?
6. Can JS Solutions materially influence the problem?
7. Is the decision maker involved?
8. Is there a credible path to justify the investment?
9. Is the required work compatible with JS Solutions capacity and margin targets?
10. What is the correct next action?

Use `discovery-call.md` for the question framework. Do not mechanically interrogate the prospect.

## 3. Business-value qualification

Do not sell $1,500/month because the prospect can technically pay $1,500/month.

Seek enough context to understand whether improvement could plausibly be valuable.

Useful inputs include:
- priority service/job;
- approximate customer/job value or lifetime value when the owner knows it;
- gross margin/business value where appropriate and voluntarily shared;
- desired number/type of additional customers;
- current lead sources;
- current conversion constraints;
- operational capacity;
- seasonality;
- geographic/service expansion goals.

Do not invent ROI from missing numbers.

If the economics cannot reasonably support the engagement, do not recommend it.

## 4. Presenting the diagnosis

Present only the most important findings.

For each:

**Evidence → Business consequence → Priority → Approach**

Connect public findings to what was learned in discovery.

Example:

> You mentioned that replacement work is the service you most want to grow. Right now the site gives that service much less depth and proof than your general repair offering. I would treat that as a higher priority than publishing broad blog content because it sits closer to the customer and revenue outcome you actually care about.

This is where the audit becomes a business plan rather than an SEO checklist.

## 5. Recommendation decision

Choose one of four outcomes.

### A. Local Growth System
Recommend the core recurring engagement when:
- there is a meaningful ongoing acquisition/optimization problem;
- JS Solutions has a credible path to influence it;
- the business can support more demand;
- the economics can justify the investment;
- the initial foundation can fit inside normal onboarding capacity.

Working price: **$1,500/month**.

### B. Growth Foundation + Local Growth System
Use when substantial one-time cleanup/build work is required before normal recurring optimization can work efficiently.

Working Foundation range: **$1,500–$3,500 one time**, scoped to actual work.

Do not create a Foundation fee merely to increase contract value.

### C. Local Growth Plus
Use when complexity/capacity requirements materially exceed the core engagement, such as multiple markets/locations or a demonstrably larger execution requirement.

Working price: **from $2,500/month**.

### D. No proposal
Use when the problem, economics, capacity, authority, timing, trust, or delivery fit does not justify an engagement.

A good sales system should produce no-proposal decisions.

## 6. Offer transition

Recommended pattern:

> Based on what you've told me and what I found, I don't think you need a pile of disconnected marketing tasks. The biggest opportunity is to continuously work the highest-value problems across the website, local search and conversion path. That's what our Local Growth System is built around.

Then explain only the relevant engagement.

Do not feature-dump every JS Solutions capability.

## 7. Pricing

For the core engagement:

> For a business like this, our Local Growth System starts at **$1,500 per month**. If the initial foundation requires a larger rebuild or cleanup, I separate that into a one-time project instead of hiding it inside the monthly fee.

Then stop and let the prospect respond.

Do not immediately discount, defend, or fill silence.

## 8. Objection handling

Objections are information. Diagnose before answering.

### “That's expensive.”

Determine whether the issue is affordability, perceived value, trust, timing, or comparison with a cheaper service.

Useful response:

> I understand. Is the concern mainly the amount itself, or that you aren't yet convinced the opportunity justifies that amount?

If economics do not support the fee, do not force the sale.

### “Can you guarantee leads/rankings?”

> No. I can commit to the work, measurement and prioritization process, but I won't guarantee rankings or a specific number of leads that we don't control.

A requirement for guarantees can be a disqualifier.

### “Can you do it cheaper?”

Do not discount automatically. Reduce scope only if a smaller engagement remains coherent and economically viable.

> I don't want to remove random pieces just to hit a number. If the full engagement doesn't make sense, I'd rather identify whether there is a smaller standalone priority that does.

### “We need to think about it.”

> Of course. What part do you need to think through — the plan, the investment, timing, or something else?

The goal is clarity, not pressure.

### “Send a proposal.”

Only send one if the business case is understood. A proposal is not a substitute for discovery.

## 9. Proposal gate

Before creating a proposal, all should be sufficiently known:
- business objective;
- priority service/customer/market;
- diagnosed opportunity;
- recommended engagement;
- decision maker/process;
- price has been discussed or will not be a surprise;
- major initial Foundation requirement known;
- success metrics identified at a reasonable level;
- no unresolved hard disqualifier.

If material information is missing, outcome is `NEEDS_MORE_INFORMATION`, not `PROPOSAL`.

## 10. Proposal construction

Use `proposal-structure.md`.

The proposal should document the business case already discussed:
1. executive summary;
2. current-state findings;
3. objectives;
4. recommended scope;
5. first 30 days;
6. investment;
7. success measurement;
8. responsibilities;
9. next step.

Do not add a large generic agency deck.

## 11. Proposal follow-up

At proposal delivery, establish the decision process rather than sending a PDF into a void.

Example:

> I'll send this over today. Does **[agreed day/time]** make sense to reconnect after you've had a chance to review it?

If no decision date can be established, record that as a sales signal.

Follow-up should answer unresolved questions or clarify the decision. Do not manufacture scarcity.

## 12. Decision states

### WON
Required before handoff:
- explicit acceptance;
- agreement/terms handled through the actual commercial process;
- billing/payment requirements satisfied as defined;
- correct scope recorded;
- onboarding owner/next action identified.

Then transition to the Client Growth System.

### LOST
Record a real reason where known:
- price/economics;
- incumbent provider;
- trust/proof;
- timing;
- no urgency;
- chose competitor;
- internal hire;
- problem not valuable enough;
- JS Solutions not fit;
- no decision;
- other known reason.

Do not invent a reason when unknown.

### NURTURE
Use only when there is a real future trigger or timing condition. Record the trigger/date. Do not use `NURTURE` as a dumping ground for dead opportunities.

### NOT FIT
Record why and stop selling.

## 13. Sales metrics

Track stage conversion rather than only wins:

**Positive responses → audits accepted → conversations → qualified opportunities → proposals → wins → MRR**

Also track:
- time from first response to audit delivery;
- audit-to-conversation rate;
- conversation-to-qualified rate;
- qualified-to-proposal rate;
- proposal-to-win rate;
- days to decision;
- lost reason;
- initial Foundation revenue;
- recurring MRR;
- eventual retention and delivery hours.

The goal is to identify the actual constraint in the revenue engine.

## 14. Integrity rules

Never:
- fabricate urgency;
- invent competitor results;
- promise rankings/leads/revenue;
- fake discounts;
- hide setup costs;
- attack an incumbent provider;
- send proposals to unqualified prospects merely to increase proposal count;
- sell work JS Solutions cannot fulfill;
- treat silence as consent;
- keep pursuing explicit opt-outs.
