# Pricing Strategy

**Status:** Sellable hypothesis v1 — validate before public package lock-in

## Decision

Use a simple initial commercial ladder rather than three arbitrary SEO packages.

| Offer | Working Price | Purpose |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| Qualified Growth Audit | Free | Earn a sales conversation with a strong-fit prospect |
| Standalone Growth Audit | $295 | Paid diagnosis when no sales qualification/engagement applies |
| Growth Foundation | $1,500–$3,500 | Correct significant foundational problems |
| Local Growth System | $1,500/mo | Core single-location recurring engagement |
| Local Growth Plus | From $2,500/mo | Higher-complexity/multi-market recurring engagement |
| Custom AI/software/major web work | Proposal | Separately scoped work |

These are initial sellable prices, not permanent market truths.

## Why $1,500/month Is the Initial Core Price

The price is high enough to support meaningful implementation and avoid the low-budget commodity-agency market, while remaining plausible for established local service businesses where one or a small number of incremental customers can have substantial economic value.

It also creates a straightforward path to the first business-model validation target:

- current ~$600 MRR + 1 core client = ~$2,100 MRR;
- current ~$600 MRR + 3 core clients = ~$5,100 MRR.

Therefore, three new core clients at the working price would cross the initial ~$5k MRR validation gate without requiring a large client count.

## Market Context

Industry pricing research consistently shows agency pricing varies widely by scope, geography, experience, and service. Retainers and project pricing are both common. This strategy therefore uses external pricing only as context; JS Solutions' actual prices must be validated against its own close rate, delivery cost, retention, and client value.

## Setup / Foundation Rule

Do not automatically charge a setup fee for every client.

Charge a Growth Foundation/setup project when the initial corrective workload is materially greater than a normal month's recurring capacity—for example, a badly structured site requiring substantial service-page, technical, analytics, or conversion work.

This prevents month one from becoming an unprofitable website-rebuild project hidden inside a $1,500 retainer.

## Minimum Economic Guardrails

### Core target

Target at least **70% contribution margin before founder overhead and general company overhead** once the delivery process is mature.

At $1,500/month, direct attributable delivery cost should ideally remain at or below roughly **$450/month**.

During the learning phase, higher founder-time cost is acceptable if it is deliberately measured and falling.

### Plus target

At $2,500/month, target direct attributable delivery cost at or below roughly **$750/month** under the same 70% contribution-margin goal.

## Internal Founder-Time Cost

For economic analysis, value founder delivery time at an initial internal planning rate of **$75/hour**.

This is not the customer billing rate. It is a management tool to prevent unpaid founder labor from making weak engagements look profitable.

At the mature 70% contribution-margin target, a $1,500 core engagement with $100/month of attributable software/AI cost leaves approximately $350/month for direct labor—about 4.7 founder-equivalent hours at the internal $75/hour rate.

This is intentionally tight. The business model depends on repeatable systems, tooling, batching, automation, and using founder attention on high-value decisions rather than manually performing every task.

## Early Learning Allowance

For the first 3–5 clients, allow up to roughly **8 founder-equivalent delivery hours per client per month** when necessary to learn the model.

Flag any client that repeatedly requires more than this. The response should be one of:

- improve the process;
- automate/delegate the work;
- reduce scope;
- move the client to Growth Plus;
- quote the work separately;
- discontinue an uneconomic engagement.

Do not normalize unlimited founder labor.

## Discounting

Do not discount merely because a prospect objects to price.

Preferred responses:

1. clarify value;
2. reduce scope;
3. phase implementation;
4. use a fixed-fee foundation project;
5. decline the engagement.

A founding-client/case-study discount may be used when the learning/proof value is explicit. Record the undiscounted price and the reason for the discount.

## Contract Strategy During Validation

Prefer low-friction commercial terms while proving the offer:

- month-to-month recurring service;
- 30-day cancellation notice;
- separate proposal/SOW for major projects;
- clear exclusions and client responsibilities.

Do not use long contracts to hide weak retention.

## When to Raise Prices

Consider raising the core price when multiple signals exist:

- healthy close rate at current price;
- strong retention;
- delivery capacity is constrained;
- client outcomes are demonstrable;
- recurring scope consistently exceeds the current economic envelope;
- proof/case studies materially improve sales confidence.

## When to Lower or Repackage

Do not automatically lower price after weak sales.

First diagnose whether the problem is targeting, proof, outreach, offer clarity, urgency, trust, or sales execution. Lower/repackage only when willingness-to-pay evidence consistently contradicts the current price and the lower price can still support acceptable economics.

## Metrics Required for Every Proposal/Client

- proposed price;
- accepted price;
- discount and reason;
- setup/project revenue;
- recurring revenue;
- founder hours;
- contractor cost;
- attributable AI/API/software cost;
- contribution margin;
- close/loss reason;
- retention;
- expansion;
- client outcome/value evidence.
