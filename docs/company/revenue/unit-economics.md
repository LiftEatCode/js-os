# Unit Economics

**Status:** Operating hypothesis v1

## Objective

Ensure growth creates profitable revenue and owner leverage rather than simply creating more founder work.

## Core Offer Economics

### Local Growth System

- Revenue: $1,500/month
- Mature contribution-margin target: >=70%
- Mature direct-cost ceiling: approximately $450/month
- Founder-time planning value: $75/hour
- Early learning allowance: up to ~8 founder-equivalent delivery hours/month
- Mature desired founder-equivalent labor: materially below the learning allowance; approximately 4–5 hours/month or less if other direct costs are near $100/month

### Local Growth Plus

- Revenue: starts at $2,500/month
- Mature contribution-margin target: >=70%
- Mature direct-cost ceiling: approximately $750/month
- Higher delivery capacity must be explicit rather than unlimited.

## Contribution Margin

**Contribution margin dollars = collected client revenue – attributable direct delivery costs**

**Contribution margin % = contribution margin dollars / collected client revenue**

Direct delivery costs include:

- founder delivery labor at internal planning cost;
- employee/contractor delivery labor;
- client-specific AI/API usage;
- client-specific software/licenses;
- attributable production/vendor costs.

General company overhead is tracked separately so client delivery economics remain visible.

## Founder Time

Use an initial internal value of **$75/hour** for founder delivery time.

This is deliberately not a customer-facing hourly rate. It exposes engagements that only appear profitable because the owner works for free.

Separate founder time into:

- sales/acquisition;
- onboarding;
- strategy;
- recurring delivery;
- client communication;
- rework/firefighting.

The desired trend is for recurring delivery and firefighting time per client to decline as systems improve.

## Example Core Engagement

At $1,500/month:

- $1,500 revenue
- $100 attributable AI/software
- 4.5 founder-equivalent hours × $75 = $337.50 labor
- $437.50 direct cost
- $1,062.50 contribution
- ~70.8% contribution margin

This is an illustrative target model, not current measured performance.

## Learning-Phase Example

At the temporary 8-hour learning allowance:

- $1,500 revenue
- $100 AI/software
- 8 hours × $75 = $600 labor
- $700 direct cost
- $800 contribution
- ~53.3% contribution margin

That can be acceptable temporarily while learning, but should trigger process improvement rather than become the permanent delivery model.

## Acquisition Economics

Track acquisition separately from delivery.

Per channel/vertical record:

- research/prospecting time;
- audit preparation cost;
- AI/API cost;
- outreach cost;
- sales-call time;
- proposal time;
- customers won.

**CAC = total attributable acquisition cost / new customers won**

Do not rely on a stable LTV:CAC ratio until enough retention history exists to estimate LTV credibly.

## Payback

Use contribution margin, not top-line revenue, when evaluating acquisition payback.

**CAC payback months = CAC / monthly contribution margin**

Early target hypothesis: keep payback comfortably below six months, with a preference for approximately three months or less once the acquisition engine matures.

## Retention Economics

Track:

- logo retention;
- MRR retention;
- average client lifetime;
- expansion revenue;
- contraction;
- reason for churn.

Do not manufacture LTV from assumed multi-year retention. Use realized cohort data as it develops.

## AI Economics

Track AI cost by useful unit where possible:

- per qualified prospect;
- per research package;
- per audit;
- per proposal;
- per client/month;
- per website optimization;
- per attributable won client.

AI is economically useful when it reduces cost/time, improves throughput/quality, improves conversion/retention, or enables valuable work that would otherwise be uneconomic.

## Company-Level Metrics

Track:

- MRR and ARR;
- new/churned/expansion MRR;
- project revenue;
- average revenue/client;
- contribution margin;
- CAC;
- CAC payback;
- retention;
- owner hours/client;
- owner hours/$1k MRR;
- AI/software cost as % of revenue;
- delivery capacity;
- pipeline value and conversion.

## Capacity Model

At the mature core target of roughly 4–5 founder-equivalent delivery hours per client/month:

- 5 clients require roughly 20–25 recurring delivery hours/month;
- 10 clients require roughly 40–50 hours/month;
- 20 clients require roughly 80–100 hours/month before additional leverage.

This illustrates why documentation, batching, automation, contractors/employees, and JS OS are strategic requirements before large client-count scale.

## Economic Escalation Rules

Flag an engagement when any of these persist:

- recurring delivery exceeds 8 founder-equivalent hours/month;
- contribution margin remains below 50% after onboarding/learning;
- repeated out-of-scope requests consume material capacity;
- client-specific software/AI cost grows without matching value;
- client cannot provide required access/input and creates repeated rework;
- revenue is static while scope continually expands.

The remedy may be process improvement, automation, repricing, scope reduction, tier migration, separate project billing, or ending the engagement.

## Validation Gate

At approximately $5,000 MRR, do not call the model validated unless JS Solutions can also show:

- multiple independently acquired clients;
- measured delivery time/cost;
- positive contribution margin;
- acceptable retention signals;
- a repeatable sales path;
- no hidden dependence on unsustainable founder labor.
