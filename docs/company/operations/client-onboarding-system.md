# Client Onboarding System v1

**Status:** Testable operating system v1

## Purpose

Define the controlled transition from a newly won JS Solutions client into active Local Growth System delivery.

The objective is not merely to collect logins and schedule a kickoff. Onboarding must preserve sales context, establish truthful business facts, obtain only necessary access, create a measurable baseline, expose blockers early, and produce the first prioritized growth plan without allowing ad hoc requests to consume the engagement.

Core flow:

**Won → Commercial Handoff → Welcome → Intake → Access → Baseline → Kickoff → 30/60/90 Plan → Growth Backlog → First Improvement → Recurring Delivery**

A client is considered onboarded only when JS Solutions has enough verified context, access, measurement, scope clarity, and prioritized work to operate responsibly.

---

## 1. Onboarding entry gate

Do not begin delivery merely because a prospect verbally says yes.

Before onboarding starts, record or confirm:
- accepted commercial scope;
- engagement tier;
- recurring price;
- Foundation scope/price if applicable;
- start date;
- billing/payment state required by the agreement;
- decision maker;
- primary working contact;
- material promises made during sales;
- known exclusions;
- known approval requirements;
- priority services/markets discussed;
- key discovery/audit findings;
- known client constraints or risks.

If commercial terms are unresolved, keep the account in sales rather than silently starting fulfillment.

---

## 2. Commercial handoff record

Sales context must survive the transition into fulfillment.

The handoff should answer:

### Business
- What does the business sell?
- Which services/customers matter most right now?
- Which markets/locations matter?
- What is the business trying to change?
- Can the business handle additional demand?
- What seasonality or operational constraints matter?

### Acquisition
- Where do customers currently come from?
- What appears to be working?
- What appears weak or underdeveloped?
- Which competitors matter?
- What measurable customer-acquisition data is already available?

### Commercial
- What did JS Solutions recommend?
- What did the client agree to buy?
- What is explicitly outside scope?
- What expectations were established about timing/results?
- What requires client approval?

### Evidence
Carry forward the relevant public audit/discovery evidence, but do not treat public assumptions as verified private facts.

---

## 3. Welcome and expectation setting

The welcome communication should make the operating model clear immediately.

Communicate:
- primary JS Solutions contact/channel;
- what happens during onboarding;
- what information/access will be requested;
- expected client responsibilities;
- how approvals work;
- how requests are handled;
- expected response times/communication cadence;
- when the first growth plan/review should be expected;
- that priorities may change as better evidence becomes available.

Key expectation:

> JS Solutions continuously prioritizes the highest-value growth work within the engagement. Client requests are important inputs, but they do not automatically become unlimited immediate work.

Do not promise arbitrary ranking, lead, revenue, or completion guarantees.

---

## 4. Client intake questionnaire

Intake should gather facts that materially affect growth decisions. Avoid bloated questionnaires that clients cannot complete accurately.

### Business identity
- public business name;
- legal business name where commercially necessary;
- primary address/location model;
- service areas;
- primary phone;
- primary customer-facing email;
- hours;
- website/domain;
- years in business;
- licenses/certifications/associations relevant to customer trust;
- owner/team facts the client authorizes for public use.

### Services and economics
- complete service list;
- top 3–5 priority services;
- services the client wants more of;
- services the client does not want to emphasize;
- typical customer/job value where known;
- repeat/LTV characteristics where known;
- margins only when client is comfortable providing them and they materially affect prioritization;
- geographic limits;
- current capacity/backlog;
- seasonal constraints.

### Customers
- ideal customer types;
- customer types/jobs to avoid;
- common customer questions;
- major objections;
- reasons customers choose the business;
- strongest proof/testimonials/case studies;
- common referral sources.

### Current acquisition
- Google/local search;
- referrals;
- repeat customers;
- paid advertising;
- social;
- directories/marketplaces;
- partnerships;
- other meaningful channels.

Ask which channels the owner believes work, while treating that answer as a hypothesis until data supports it.

### Current systems/vendors
- website platform/host;
- analytics;
- Search Console;
- Google Business Profile;
- CRM/shop management/booking system;
- call tracking;
- advertising platforms;
- social accounts;
- email platform;
- current/previous agencies/vendors;
- reputation/review tools;
- repository/deployment platform where applicable.

### History
- major website rebuilds/migrations;
- prior SEO work;
- prior penalties/manual actions known to client;
- advertising history;
- major marketing successes/failures;
- domain/business-name/location changes;
- known tracking problems.

### Goals
Ask for business outcomes, not only marketing outputs.

Examples:
- more brake jobs;
- more HVAC replacements;
- fill underused service capacity;
- expand into a nearby market;
- improve quality of inbound leads;
- reduce dependence on paid leads;
- launch a new service line.

## 5. Business-fact verification

Client-submitted facts are inputs, not automatic permission to publish.

Classify material facts as:
- **VERIFIED** — confirmed through client-owned source/credible evidence;
- **CLIENT_CONFIRMED** — explicitly confirmed by authorized client contact;
- **PUBLICLY_OBSERVED** — observed publicly but not privately confirmed;
- **UNKNOWN** — not established;
- **CONFLICTING** — sources disagree and require resolution.

Never invent or silently normalize:
- licenses;
- years in business;
- warranties;
- financing;
- locations;
- service areas;
- prices;
- awards;
- credentials;
- customer counts;
- review claims;
- business ownership/history.

Consequential public facts require appropriate confirmation before publication.

---

## 6. Access checklist

Request only access required by approved scope.

### Website / infrastructure
When applicable:
- CMS/admin access;
- GitHub/repository access;
- hosting/deployment platform;
- domain/DNS only when necessary;
- CDN/security platform where relevant.

### Google / measurement
When applicable:
- Google Analytics;
- Google Search Console;
- Google Business Profile;
- Google Ads only if in scope;
- Tag Manager where used.

### Leads / operations
When applicable:
- form destination/configuration;
- call tracking;
- booking system;
- CRM;
- shop/service-management platform where approved and necessary.

### Marketing/reputation
Only when in scope:
- social platforms;
- review/reputation system;
- email marketing;
- advertising accounts;
- directory/listing systems.

### Access rules
- prefer delegated/user access over shared credentials;
- use least privilege;
- do not request personal passwords when account invitation is possible;
- do not store secrets in ordinary client notes/docs;
- record who granted access and what level was granted;
- record missing/blocking access;
- remove/review access when engagement ends or scope changes.

Broad technical credentials do not create broad operating authorization.

---

## 7. Access status model

For each required system record:
- system;
- reason needed;
- required permission level;
- status;
- requested date;
- granted date;
- account owner;
- blocker/notes;
- whether it blocks baseline, implementation, measurement, or none.

Suggested statuses:
- NOT_REQUIRED;
- NOT_REQUESTED;
- REQUESTED;
- PARTIAL;
- GRANTED;
- BLOCKED;
- INVALID;
- REVOKED.

Do not hold the entire onboarding hostage to non-critical access. Proceed with what can be responsibly established and clearly record limitations.

---

## 8. Baseline procedure

Baseline exists so JS Solutions can distinguish the starting condition from later changes.

Do not invent historical baselines when data is unavailable.

### Business baseline
Record where available:
- priority services;
- target markets;
- current capacity;
- average job/customer value;
- known close/book rate;
- current acquisition mix;
- seasonality;
- owner-defined business objective.

### Visibility baseline
Record where available:
- Search Console demand/click/query patterns;
- priority service/query visibility observations;
- indexed architecture;
- GBP/local visibility and completeness;
- major competitor positioning;
- service/location coverage gaps.

### Trust baseline
Record:
- review profile/velocity where observable;
- testimonials/case studies;
- owner/team proof;
- credentials/licensing presentation;
- warranties/financing/offers where verified;
- consistency of business information;
- major trust friction.

### Conversion baseline
Record:
- primary CTAs;
- call/form/booking paths;
- tracked conversions where available;
- mobile friction;
- service-page/customer-journey friction;
- lead-routing failures;
- obvious abandonment/confusion risks.

### Technical/measurement baseline
Record:
- analytics health;
- conversion event health;
- Search Console state;
- crawl/indexation issues;
- major performance/reliability problems;
- metadata/schema/internal-linking issues when relevant;
- broken forms/routes/tracking.

For each baseline claim classify the evidence as measured fact, observed fact, reasonable inference, or unknown.

---

## 9. Validate the sales diagnosis

The public prospect audit was a sales diagnostic, not final truth.

After access/intake, review every material sales finding and classify it:
- CONFIRMED;
- PARTIALLY_CONFIRMED;
- DISPROVEN;
- STILL_UNKNOWN;
- SUPERSEDED_BY_HIGHER_PRIORITY_FINDING.

Do not execute work merely because it appeared in the sales audit.

Private data may change the diagnosis.

This is an important trust rule: JS Solutions should be willing to tell the client when new evidence shows that an earlier hypothesis was wrong or less important than expected.

---

## 10. Kickoff meeting

A kickoff should normally occur after enough intake/access has been collected to make the conversation useful.

Target: approximately 30–45 minutes unless client complexity requires more.

### Kickoff objectives
1. Confirm business priorities.
2. Resolve material intake conflicts/unknowns.
3. Confirm operational capacity.
4. Review access blockers.
5. Confirm how leads/conversions should be understood.
6. Confirm communication and approvals.
7. Explain initial diagnosis and likely first priorities.
8. Surface major upcoming events, seasonality, launches, promotions, or constraints.
9. Confirm who can approve consequential changes.
10. Establish the next milestone.

Do not turn kickoff into a generic presentation about JS Solutions.

---

## 11. 30/60/90 growth plan

After baseline and kickoff, create a client-specific operating plan.

### Days 1–30 — Establish and correct
Priorities typically include:
- complete critical access;
- repair measurement/lead-routing problems;
- validate audit assumptions;
- fix high-confidence technical/conversion/trust blockers;
- establish the growth backlog;
- implement the strongest quick wins within scope;
- define the most important hypotheses for the next 60 days.

### Days 31–60 — Strengthen priority journeys
Potential work:
- priority service/customer-intent improvements;
- local/GBP work where authorized;
- on-page/service architecture;
- internal linking/schema;
- trust/reputation improvements;
- conversion improvements;
- content tied to business priorities;
- competitor/customer-journey gaps;
- measurement refinement.

### Days 61–90 — Expand evidence-backed work
Potential work:
- expand what appears to work;
- deeper service/location opportunities;
- conversion tests;
- reputation/local authority systems;
- remove low-value assumptions/backlog items;
- establish stable recurring optimization cadence.

The plan is directional and prioritized. It is not a promise to complete every item by an arbitrary date.

---

## 12. Initial growth backlog

Every meaningful onboarding finding should either:
- become a backlog item;
- be explicitly rejected/deferred;
- be marked informational only;
- become an access/client-input blocker.

Recommended backlog fields:
- title/opportunity;
- category;
- evidence/source;
- business objective;
- expected mechanism/value;
- impact;
- confidence;
- effort;
- risk;
- revenue relevance where reasonably inferable;
- approval requirement;
- dependencies;
- scope/tier fit;
- status;
- planned timing;
- implementation evidence;
- validation result;
- measured result/learning.

Suggested categories:
- VISIBILITY;
- TRUST;
- CONVERSION;
- MEASUREMENT;
- TECHNICAL;
- CONTENT;
- CLIENT_REQUEST;
- SPECIAL_PROJECT.

Prioritize expected business value under available evidence, risk, and engagement capacity—not ease of reporting.

---

## 13. Client requests during onboarding

New clients often begin sending requests immediately.

Do not allow this to destroy the onboarding process.

Classify each request:

### Urgent operational blocker
Examples: broken lead form, materially wrong public business information, site outage.

Evaluate immediately.

### Normal growth request
Capture in backlog and prioritize against existing work.

### Consequential change
Requires appropriate approval and validation.

### Out-of-scope / special project
Explain the boundary and decide whether to trade capacity, use Plus, or quote separately.

### Low-value request
Explain why it is not currently prioritized when appropriate.

Client responsiveness matters; unlimited task-taking is not the service model.

---

## 14. Approval map

During onboarding identify authorized approvers for:
- public business facts;
- website copy/brand changes;
- pricing/offers;
- legal/licensing claims;
- major design/architecture changes;
- production deployments where client approval is required;
- GBP/account changes;
- advertising/spend;
- customer communications;
- scope/budget changes.

Approval authorizes the defined action; it does not imply authorization for unrelated future actions.

---

## 15. Communication model

Establish a simple default communication system.

### Routine
Use the agreed working channel for:
- required client input;
- approval requests;
- meaningful progress/issues;
- scheduled review.

### Avoid
- constant micro-update noise;
- reporting every tiny technical edit;
- multiple conflicting request channels when avoidable;
- allowing text/email/chat requests to bypass prioritization/scope control.

### Escalate promptly
- lead-routing failure;
- site outage/material regression;
- security/access issue;
- materially inaccurate public business information;
- unexpected paid-spend risk;
- legal/reputation-sensitive issue;
- client action blocking critical work.

---

## 16. First meaningful improvement

Onboarding should lead to action, not weeks of paperwork.

Target the first meaningful improvement as soon as enough evidence/access exists to do so safely.

A meaningful improvement:
- addresses a real customer-acquisition constraint;
- has evidence behind it;
- fits approved scope;
- can be validated;
- is not selected merely because it is easy.

Track:
- days from engagement start to baseline;
- days to first implemented improvement;
- what the improvement addressed;
- expected mechanism;
- validation/result.

---

## 17. Onboarding completion gate

Do not mark onboarding complete based only on elapsed time.

Minimum completion criteria:
- commercial handoff recorded;
- key business facts captured with uncertainty visible;
- critical access granted or blockers documented;
- measurable baseline established to the extent data allows;
- public audit findings revalidated;
- business goals/priority services confirmed;
- approval map established;
- communication expectations established;
- initial 30/60/90 plan created;
- initial growth backlog created/prioritized;
- first meaningful improvement selected or executed;
- Foundation/out-of-scope needs identified;
- delivery economics tracking started.

If important data remains unavailable, onboarding may still complete with explicit limitations if recurring work can proceed responsibly.

---

## 18. Foundation escalation gate

During onboarding, determine whether the existing digital foundation prevents normal recurring optimization.

Potential triggers:
- major website rebuild required;
- broken/unmaintainable architecture;
- severe tracking reconstruction;
- substantial migration;
- major service/information architecture rebuild;
- large one-time technical cleanup beyond normal monthly capacity.

If triggered, define a Growth Foundation with:
- problem;
- deliverables;
- completion criteria;
- dependencies;
- approvals;
- price;
- relationship to recurring work.

Do not invent a Foundation fee simply because a new client is onboarding.

---

## 19. Onboarding economics

Track onboarding separately from recurring delivery so first-month effort does not disappear inside average monthly estimates.

Record:
- founder-equivalent onboarding hours;
- AI/software/direct cost;
- access/admin time;
- baseline/audit time;
- kickoff time;
- implementation time;
- client-delay time;
- rework caused by missing/incorrect information.

For the first 3–5 standardized clients, learning is expected. The purpose is to discover what can be templated, automated, eliminated, or moved into Foundation/Plus.

Repeated excessive onboarding effort is evidence that the offer, process, pricing, or qualification gate needs adjustment.

---

## 20. Onboarding risk signals

Escalate when:
- client cannot provide/authorize critical access;
- decision maker and working contact disagree materially;
- client cannot identify priority services/goals;
- business cannot handle additional demand;
- supplied facts are repeatedly inaccurate;
- client requests deceptive tactics;
- client expects guaranteed rankings/leads;
- reputation/operational issues materially undermine growth work;
- scope expected by client differs from sold scope;
- existing vendor conflict prevents responsible execution;
- measurement cannot be established and client rejects reasonable alternatives;
- client immediately demonstrates chronic scope expansion.

Onboarding is also a final fit-validation stage. A signed client can still become an economically or ethically unsuitable engagement.

## 22. First-client readiness checklist

Before accepting the next standardized Local Growth System client, JS Solutions should be able to answer yes to the following:

### Commercial
- Is the offer/scope documented?
- Is the price documented?
- Are Foundation/Plus escalation rules defined?
- Are exclusions clear?

### Sales handoff
- Can discovery/audit/proposal context be transferred without relying on founder memory?

### Intake
- Is there a defined set of business/service/customer questions?
- Can unknown/conflicting facts remain explicitly unknown?

### Access
- Do we know which systems may be required and why?
- Are least-privilege/delegated-access rules defined?

### Delivery
- Can we create a baseline?
- Can we create a 30/60/90 plan?
- Can we capture/prioritize a backlog?
- Can client requests be controlled through that backlog?

### Measurement
- Can we record starting conditions and conversion/lead signals where available?
- Can we avoid making unsupported attribution claims?

### Economics
- Can we track onboarding and recurring founder-equivalent time separately?
- Can we identify when scope no longer fits the tier?

### Communication / approvals
- Do we know who can approve consequential actions?
- Does the client understand how requests/priorities work?

If these controls are in place, JS Solutions is operationally ready to onboard the next client even though the system will continue improving from real delivery evidence.

# Core principle

> Onboarding converts a sale into a controlled operating relationship.

The output is not “accounts connected.”

The output is:

**Verified context + appropriate access + measurable baseline + clear approvals + prioritized growth plan + economically controlled delivery.**
