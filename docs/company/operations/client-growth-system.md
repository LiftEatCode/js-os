# Client Growth System v1

**Status:** Operating hypothesis v1

## Purpose

Define what happens after a JS Solutions prospect becomes a client so that sales promises turn into controlled, measurable, economically sustainable delivery.

The lifecycle is:

**Won → Commercial Handoff → Onboarding → Access → Baseline → Growth Plan → Prioritized Backlog → Execute → Validate → Measure → Review → Repeat**

The client is buying an ongoing customer-acquisition optimization system, not a fixed quota of SEO tasks.

## 1. Won-to-delivery handoff

Do not begin work from memory or from the proposal alone.

Create a handoff record containing:
- client/business identity;
- decision maker and working contacts;
- signed/accepted scope;
- recurring price;
- Foundation scope/price if applicable;
- billing status/start date;
- priority services/customers/markets from discovery;
- business goals;
- capacity constraints;
- key audit findings;
- known risks/limitations;
- promised first actions;
- required access;
- success measures discussed;
- communication expectations;
- exclusions/approval requirements.

Sales context must survive the handoff.

## 2. Onboarding

### Client intake

Confirm:
- legal/public business name as needed;
- locations/service areas;
- services and priority services;
- primary phone/email/contact paths;
- hours;
- licenses/credentials where relevant;
- brand assets;
- offers/financing/warranties where relevant;
- customer types to prioritize/avoid;
- operational capacity;
- seasonality;
- competitors the client cares about;
- existing vendors/agencies;
- major website/marketing history.

Do not overwrite public business facts based on assumptions.

### Access inventory

Request only access actually needed for the approved scope.

Potential systems:
- website/CMS;
- domain/DNS where necessary;
- hosting/deployment;
- Google Analytics;
- Google Search Console;
- Google Business Profile where authorized;
- call tracking/forms/CRM where applicable;
- advertising platforms only if in scope;
- repository/GitHub where applicable;
- social/reputation platforms only if in scope.

Use least privilege. Never request a client's personal password when delegated/user access is available.

Record missing/blocking access separately from completed access.

## 3. Baseline

Before claiming improvement, establish what can actually be measured.

Baseline categories:

### Business
- priority services;
- target markets;
- lead/customer value where client can provide it;
- capacity;
- current acquisition sources;
- known close/book rate where available.

### Visibility
- priority query/service visibility where measurable;
- Search Console impressions/clicks/queries;
- GBP performance where available/authorized;
- indexed/service/location architecture;
- major competitor presence.

### Trust
- review profile and velocity;
- first-party proof/testimonials;
- credentials/licensing presentation;
- owner/team/experience proof;
- consistency of public business information.

### Conversion
- primary calls/forms/bookings;
- website conversion tracking;
- CTA/service journey quality;
- mobile/customer friction;
- lead-routing failures.

### Technical
- crawl/indexation issues;
- performance/reliability issues that materially affect the customer/search journey;
- analytics/tracking health;
- major metadata/schema/internal-linking problems where relevant.

Mark unavailable data as unavailable. Do not backfill invented baselines.

## 4. 30/60/90 growth plan

Create a prioritized plan based on actual business value and evidence.

### First 30 days
Primary goals:
- establish access and measurement;
- validate sales/audit assumptions with private data;
- fix high-confidence trust/conversion/technical blockers;
- establish the prioritized backlog;
- execute the best quick wins that fit capacity;
- define 60/90-day hypotheses.

### Days 31–60
Typical focus:
- priority service/customer-intent improvements;
- local-search/GBP work where justified and authorized;
- internal linking/schema/on-page improvements;
- reputation/proof improvements;
- conversion improvements;
- measurement refinement;
- competitor/customer-journey gaps.

### Days 61–90
Typical focus:
- expand what evidence suggests is working;
- address deeper service/location/content opportunities;
- test conversion improvements;
- strengthen local authority/reputation systems;
- remove low-value backlog items;
- establish the recurring optimization cadence.

The plan is not a promise that every listed task will be completed by an arbitrary date. It is a prioritized operating plan constrained by engagement capacity and new evidence.

## 5. Growth backlog

Every meaningful opportunity should become a backlog item with enough context to make a decision.

Recommended fields:
- opportunity;
- source/evidence;
- business objective;
- expected mechanism/value;
- impact: High/Medium/Low;
- confidence: High/Medium/Low;
- effort: High/Medium/Low;
- risk;
- approval requirement;
- dependencies;
- status;
- outcome/measurement after execution.

Prioritization principle:

**Highest expected business value under available evidence and delivery capacity.**

Do not prioritize work because it is easy to count in a monthly report.

## 6. Monthly execution loop

Each cycle:

**Observe → Diagnose → Prioritize → Execute → Validate → Measure → Learn → Reprioritize**

### Observe
Review business/search/website/reputation/conversion/competitor signals.

### Diagnose
Determine what changed, what is limiting progress, and what opportunities now matter most.

### Prioritize
Select work based on impact, confidence, effort, risk, business relevance, and capacity.

### Execute
Perform approved work within engagement scope/capacity.

### Validate
Confirm implementation quality and that changes did not create regressions.

### Measure
Use available reliable data. Distinguish correlation from causation.

### Learn
Record what the result changes about future priorities.

### Reprioritize
Update the backlog rather than blindly continuing an annual checklist.

## 7. Core Local Growth System delivery

Working recurring price: **$1,500/month**.

The engagement may include, according to priority and capacity:
- monthly business/search/website review;
- prioritized growth backlog;
- technical/on-page SEO;
- local SEO/GBP work where authorized;
- service/content improvements;
- internal linking/schema/metadata;
- conversion/CTA improvements;
- reputation/review support;
- competitor monitoring;
- analytics/lead measurement;
- business-focused review/report;
- implementation of the highest-priority approved work within engagement capacity.

This is not unlimited labor and not a fixed monthly quota of pages, posts, links, or changes.

## 8. Growth Foundation

Working range: **$1,500–$3,500 one time**.

Use only when onboarding reveals substantial one-time work beyond normal first-month capacity, such as a major website foundation/rebuild, tracking reconstruction, architecture cleanup, or other clearly scoped prerequisite.

Foundation must have:
- explicit problem;
- deliverables;
- completion criteria;
- price;
- dependencies;
- approvals;
- relationship to recurring work.

Do not use Foundation as a vague setup fee.

## 9. Local Growth Plus

Working price: **from $2,500/month**.

Use when the client's complexity materially requires more recurring capacity, such as multiple locations/markets, materially larger content/service architecture, broader conversion experimentation, or another justified scope increase.

Price follows complexity/capacity, not client prestige.

## 10. Change and approval control

Not every technically possible optimization should be executed automatically.

Changes may require client approval when they affect:
- public business facts;
- pricing/offers;
- legal/licensing claims;
- major brand/messaging direction;
- website redesign/restructure;
- consequential production deployment;
- paid spend;
- third-party account settings;
- customer communications;
- scope/budget.

Routine approved-scope changes may become progressively more delegated as trust, tooling, policy, and JS OS autonomy mature.

## 11. Monthly client review

The review should answer:

1. What changed in the business/customer-acquisition system?
2. What did JS Solutions do and why?
3. What evidence/result do we have?
4. What did we learn?
5. What are the next highest priorities?
6. What decisions/access/input are needed from the client?

Avoid activity theater such as reporting a long list of tiny edits without business context.

Useful metrics vary by client and may include:
- qualified calls/forms/bookings;
- conversion rate;
- lead attribution quality;
- priority service visibility;
- Search Console demand/click trends;
- GBP/search engagement where reliable;
- reputation/review indicators;
- agreed business outcomes.

Never guarantee causality from noisy marketing data.

## 12. Delivery economics

The service must create value for the client and remain economically viable for JS Solutions.

Current planning targets:
- mature contribution margin target: **>=70%** before founder/general overhead;
- at $1,500/month, attributable direct delivery cost ideally **<= ~$450/month**;
- at $2,500/month, ideally **<= ~$750/month**;
- internal founder planning rate: **$75/hour**;
- early learning clients may temporarily use roughly **up to 8 founder-equivalent hours/client/month**;
- mature core engagement should trend toward roughly **4–5 founder-equivalent hours/month or less**, depending on actual AI/software/direct costs.

Escalate when:
- recurring work repeatedly exceeds roughly 8 founder-equivalent hours;
- contribution margin remains below ~50% after the learning period;
- scope creep is recurring;
- client approvals/access consume disproportionate time;
- the engagement requires capacity inconsistent with its tier.

Options are reprioritize, rescope, automate, raise tier/price, or end an economically broken engagement. Do not hide the problem.

## 13. Retention and expansion

Retention should come from continued value, not artificial lock-in.

Potential expansion signals:
- additional location/market;
- new high-value service line;
- materially greater execution capacity required;
- paid acquisition need;
- automation/software opportunity;
- major website rebuild;
- deeper analytics/CRM integration.

Expansion must solve a real business problem and be separately scoped when outside the recurring engagement.

## 14. Client-risk signals

Escalate when:
- business cannot handle leads;
- client repeatedly supplies inaccurate information;
- reputation/operational problems undermine marketing;
- access is chronically blocked;
- client demands deceptive tactics or guarantees;
- no measurable business objective can be established;
- client repeatedly expands scope without commercial adjustment;
- work no longer fits JS Solutions competency/economics.

Growth marketing cannot permanently compensate for broken operations.

## 15. First-client validation metrics

For the next clients, explicitly record:
- onboarding hours;
- recurring delivery hours;
- AI/software/direct cost;
- gross/contribution margin estimate;
- time to baseline;
- time to first implemented improvement;
- number/type of backlog opportunities;
- client approval delays;
- measurable lead/conversion/search changes;
- client retention;
- expansion/contraction;
- reasons for churn if it occurs.

This data determines whether the offer is actually repeatable.

## 16. Future JS OS contract

This manual system should become the operating specification for Client Operations in JS OS.

Future agents may:
- monitor approved data sources;
- detect opportunities/anomalies;
- prepare prioritized backlog items;
- recommend work;
- prepare website/SEO changes;
- validate implementations;
- prepare monthly reviews;
- calculate delivery economics;
- surface scope/risk/capacity issues.

Agents do not gain permission merely because they can reason about an action. External/account/production actions remain governed by tools, permissions, approvals, policies, auditability, and progressive autonomy.
